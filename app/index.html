Shield

This moves test coins from your wallet into the pool. You get a private note that lives in this browser. People can see that you deposited. They cannot see the note afterwards.

Private send

Send part of a note to a shielded address. The chain shows that a transfer happened, not the amount or who received it. You keep the rest as a new note. Building the proof happens on this device and can take a minute, longer on a phone.

Send this encrypted note to the recipient through a private chat. The chain does not carry it. It is not a wallet address.

Withdraw

Turn a private note back into test coins at a normal wallet address. That address and the amount become public. Anything you do not withdraw stays in a new private note.

Notes

What shielding means

  1. A normal wallet is a glass box. Anyone can see the balance and every payment.
  2. Shielding puts test coins into a shared pool. You receive a private note, like a sealed envelope that says the amount is yours. The pool records the seal, not the amount.
  3. A private send gives that envelope to someone else's shielded address. Outsiders can see that a transfer happened. They do not see the amount or the recipient.
  4. Withdraw opens the envelope and sends the test coins to a normal address. That part is public again.
  5. Your notes stay in this browser, locked by a signature from your wallet. Export a backup. Clearing the browser deletes them.

The coins are play money. The cryptography has not been audited. Do not use a wallet that holds funds you care about.
